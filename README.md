## Introduction

Automated Decision Making (ADM) is when computer systems are used to make or help make decisions. Governments use ADM in areas such as social services, migration and public administration. It can make decisions faster and reduce the amount of manual work. However, it can also create problems involving fairness, privacy and accountability.

This e-portfolio explores six artefacts about ADM in Australia and the United Kingdom. It focuses on how automated systems affect society and why ICT professionals need to think about ethical issues when developing and using these technologies.

## Artefact 2 – Human Rights and Automated Decisions
<img width="1052" height="662" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/75ac5a5e-fedf-4279-9c73-087a938ce16a" />


**Source:** Australian Human Rights Commission (2025)

**Article:** [Use of automated decision making by government](https://humanrights.gov.au/resource-hub/by-resource-type/submissions/use-of-automated-decision-making-by-government)

### Summary of the artefact

This submission from the Australian Human Rights Commission discusses the risks of using ADM in government services. It explains that automated systems can improve efficiency, but they can also affect people's rights if there are not enough safeguards. The Commission recommends stronger protection, including human rights assessments, clear explanations of automated decisions and opportunities for people to challenge decisions that affect them.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because I think fairness is one of the most important issues with ADM. If a government system makes an incorrect decision, it could seriously affect someone's life. This connects to Kantianism, which focuses on treating people with respect and dignity. I learned that governments should not only focus on making decisions quickly. They should also make sure that people are treated fairly and have a way to question decisions.

## Artefact 3 – Responsible Use of AI in Australian Government
<img width="1345" height="796" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/79a99bf5-c630-4b7e-9f0d-b0c31bd47772" />

**Source:** Digital Transformation Agency (2026)

**Article:** [AI policy update: Strengthening responsible use across government](https://www.dta.gov.au/articles/ai-policy-update-strengthening-responsible-use-across-government)

### Summary of the artefact

This article explains updates to Australia's policy for the responsible use of artificial intelligence in government. The policy requires agencies to consider risks before using AI systems. These include fairness, privacy, safety, security and transparency. It also introduces stronger requirements for recording AI use and making sure that someone is responsible for each system. The aim is to help government agencies use AI safely while maintaining public trust.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this article because it shows that ethical issues should be considered before technology is introduced, not only after something goes wrong. I think it is important that government agencies take responsibility for the systems they use. This connects to the ACS Code of Professional Conduct, especially competence and the primacy of the public interest. It helped me understand that ICT professionals need to consider possible risks and not just whether a system works technically.

I chose this artefact because Centrelink decisions can affect people's financial support and everyday lives. An incorrect automated decision could create serious difficulties for someone who depends on government payments. I think human checking is especially important in these situations. This connects to Act Utilitarianism because we need to consider both the benefits and possible harm of automated decisions. The artefact helped me understand why proper testing and regular reviews are important even after a system has been introduced.

## Artefact 5 – Algorithmic Transparency in the United Kingdom
<img width="729" height="525" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/eaf9f0e0-43e3-4fa2-8c71-9d763acb247a" />


**Source:** UK Government (2025)

**Article:** [Publish a record of algorithmic tools using the ATRS](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/publish-a-record-of-algorithmic-tools-using-the-atrs)

### Summary of the artefact

This UK Government guidance explains the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard, also called ATRS. It requires central government organisations to publish information about certain algorithmic tools that influence decisions affecting the public. The purpose is to make the use of these tools more open and understandable. It also helps the public learn about how government departments use algorithms in their work.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because I wanted to compare how another country deals with ADM. I think the UK's approach to publishing information about algorithms is useful because it encourages transparency. When people understand how technology is being used, they may have more trust in government decisions. This connects to the ACS principle of honesty. It also made me think about how governments in Australia and other countries can learn from each other's approaches to responsible technology.

## Artefact 6 – Predictive Analytics in the UK Child Maintenance Service
<img width="739" height="540" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1d256db4-de72-4f88-ab62-32ef7834c345" />


**Source:** UK Department for Work and Pensions (2025)

**Article:** [Child Maintenance Service – Predictive Analytics Compliance](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/dwp-child-maintenance-service-cms-predictive-analytics-compliance)

### Summary of the artefact

This artefact explains how the UK's Child Maintenance Service uses predictive analytics to help caseworkers identify cases where payments may stop. The system uses past and current information to estimate the risk of payment problems. It then gives caseworkers information to help them decide which cases may need attention. The system supports workers rather than making the final decision for them.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because it shows a practical example of ADM being used to support government workers. I think this is an interesting approach because technology can help people work more efficiently without completely replacing human judgement. However, I also think there should be checks to make sure the predictions are fair and accurate. This connects to the ACS Code because ICT professionals need to consider the public interest and possible harm. It helped me understand that human oversight is important even when automated systems are only providing recommendations.

## Final Reflection

Before studying this topic, I mainly thought that automated systems were used to make work faster and easier. After learning about ADM, I have a better understanding of the ethical issues that come with using technology in government decisions.

The Australian examples showed me the importance of fairness, privacy and accountability. The UK examples helped me understand how transparency and human oversight can be used in government systems.

One thing I found important is that technology can work correctly from a technical point of view but still create unfair outcomes for people.

As a future ICT professional, I think it is important to consider how technology affects society, not just whether a system is working as expected. I believe ADM can be useful, but it should be properly tested, monitored and explained to the people affected by it.
